Our Works / Performance Marketing / Patang Toys
Kids &amp; toys · Performance Marketing

ROAS from 1.9× to 5.2× across two seasons

Building a seasonal paid acquisition plan for a toy brand whose entire year turns on eleven weeks.

Client
Patang Toys
Industry
Children’s toys · D2C
Channels
Meta Ads, Google Ads
Duration
8 months
Objective
Profitable peak-season scale
01 · The challenge

Eleven weeks of revenue, twelve months of cost

Patang made most of its year in the festive and gifting weeks, and spent those weeks acquiring cold audiences at the most expensive moment of the year. Campaigns were switched off in the quiet months, so every season started from nothing.

  • Spend concentrated entirely in the eleven highest-cost weeks of the year
  • No retargeting pool at the start of each season, so everything was prospecting
  • Creative produced in-season under time pressure
  • Stock planning disconnected from what the campaigns were pushing
02 · Campaign strategy

Spend in the quiet months, harvest in the loud ones

The plan moved a deliberate share of the budget into the off-season to build an audience the peak campaigns could retarget rather than discover.

Audience
Parents and gift-buyers by child age band, built in the off-season and harvested in peak
Offer
Age-matched gift guides off-season; bundles and delivery guarantees in peak
Creative
Play-pattern video shot in advance, tested off-season at low cost
Meta AdsGoogle ShoppingGoogle SearchCohort and stock reporting
Step 01 Ad

Off-season: age-band gift guides at a low cost per engaged visitor. Peak: bundles to the pool already built.

Step 02 Landing page

An age-filtered guide off-season; a bundle page with a delivery-by date in peak.

Step 03 Action

Off-season visitors join a segment by child age band; peak visitors buy.

Step 04 Conversion

Purchase matched back to acquisition season, so off-season spend is credited properly.

03 · Execution

What actually went live.

01

Campaign structure

A year-round build campaign at a modest budget, plus a peak campaign that spends almost entirely against the audience the build campaign created.

02

Creative

Play-pattern video shot and tested in the quiet months, so peak-season creative was already proven when it mattered.

03

Landing pages

Age-band gift guides year-round; bundle pages with a delivery-by date during the peak weeks.

04

Measurement

Purchases attributed back to the season in which the customer was acquired, so off-season spend is not judged on same-week revenue.

04 · Optimisation

Decision, reason, outcome.

Decision

Moved roughly a fifth of annual budget into the off-season

Reason

Cost per engaged visitor off-season was a fraction of the peak-week figure.

Outcome

Peak season opened with a large warm pool instead of none.

Decision

Tested all peak creative off-season

Reason

In-season testing wastes the most expensive impressions on learning.

Outcome

Peak campaigns launched with proven creative and no learning phase.

Decision

Segmented by child age band rather than by product

Reason

Gift-buyers shop by age, not by category.

Outcome

Higher click-through rate and a much better match between ad and landing page.

Decision

Capped spend on out-of-stock lines

Reason

A meaningful share of peak spend had been driving traffic to unavailable products.

Outcome

Budget redirected to in-stock lines within the same week.

06 · Business result

A season that compounds instead of restarting

The off-season budget is not a cost against the quiet months. It is the thing that makes the peak weeks affordable.

Peak revenue more than tripled on a roughly similar total annual spend, because the expensive weeks were spent harvesting rather than prospecting.

Connecting campaign budget to stock availability removed a recurring waste that had gone unnoticed in previous seasons.

A seasonal business cannot afford to meet its customers for the first time during its most expensive weeks. Moving a fifth of the budget forward was what made the peak weeks profitable.
