Our Works / Performance Marketing / Arka Finserv
Financial services · Performance Marketing

Cost per approved application down 44%

Moving a lending brand’s paid acquisition from applications started to applications actually approved.

Client
Arka Finserv
Industry
Consumer lending
Channels
Google Ads, Meta Ads
Duration
7 months
Objective
Approved applications
01 · The challenge

Paying for applications that were always going to fail

Arka was acquiring applications at a competitive cost and approving fewer than one in five. The campaigns had no visibility of approval, so they optimised for the audience most likely to start an application and least likely to pass underwriting.

  • Approval rate at 18%, with no approval signal reaching either platform
  • Eligibility criteria appeared only after six form steps
  • One campaign covered four loan products with very different criteria
  • Applicants who were declined received no alternative, so the acquisition cost was wasted entirely
02 · Campaign strategy

Advertise the criteria, not just the rate

Stating eligibility in the ad costs clicks and saves applications. We then fed approvals back so the platforms could find the audience that passes.

Audience
Salaried applicants matching each product’s income and credit criteria, segmented by product
Offer
Indicative rate with eligibility stated up front and a sixty-second eligibility check
Creative
Plain-language criteria and a worked repayment example
Google SearchMeta AdsOffline conversion importUnderwriting data feed
Step 01 Ad

Minimum income and credit criteria stated in the ad copy itself.

Step 02 Landing page

A sixty-second eligibility check before any document upload or personal detail.

Step 03 Action

Eligible applicants continue; ineligible ones are routed to a product that fits or to guidance.

Step 04 Conversion

Approval imported back as an offline conversion, so bidding targets approved applicants.

03 · Execution

What actually went live.

01

Campaign structure

Split one campaign into four, one per loan product, each with its own criteria in the copy and its own eligibility check.

02

Creative

Replaced rate-led headlines with criteria-led ones and a worked repayment example, which reduced clicks and raised approval rate.

03

Landing pages

A sixty-second eligibility check placed ahead of document upload, so ineligible applicants never reach a step that costs the business money.

04

Measurement

Approval and disbursal imported daily from underwriting as offline conversions.

04 · Optimisation

Decision, reason, outcome.

Decision

Put minimum income in the ad copy

Reason

Just over half of declines were on income criteria that were never stated before the application.

Outcome

Click volume fell; approval rate rose within the first month.

Decision

Optimised for approval rather than application start

Reason

Platforms were finding high-intent, low-eligibility applicants very efficiently.

Outcome

Cost per application rose, cost per approved application fell by 44%.

Decision

Routed declined applicants to a product that fits

Reason

A declined applicant had already been paid for and was being discarded.

Outcome

A measurable share now convert on a secured product instead.

Decision

Paused the broadest keyword set

Reason

Generic loan queries converted at a fifth the approval rate of product-specific ones.

Outcome

Budget moved to product-specific queries at a better approved cost.

06 · Business result

Twice the approved book on a similar budget

Monthly spend rose modestly while approved applications roughly doubled, because the campaigns stopped paying for people who could never be approved.

Underwriting workload per approval fell sharply, since far fewer applications now reach manual review only to be declined.

Stating criteria in the ad also reduced complaints about the application experience, which had been a recurring compliance concern.

Key takeaway

In lending, the conversion that matters sits inside underwriting rather than inside the ad platform. Feeding approvals back and stating criteria up front changed which audience the campaigns went looking for.
