Giving a residential developer a locality-led search presence instead of one page per project.

01 · The challenge

One page per project, nothing per locality

Buyers search a locality long before they search a developer. Nilaya had a page for each of its nine projects and nothing at all for the fourteen localities those projects sit in.

  • Portals outranked the developer on its own project names
  • Project pages went dark as soon as a project sold out, losing all accumulated authority
  • No locality, pricing or connectivity content of any kind
  • Google Business Profiles for the sales offices were unclaimed
02 · SEO strategy

Rank the locality, route to the project

Locality pages outlive projects. Building them gave the site an asset that keeps its authority when a project completes and sells out.

01

Locality as the unit

Fourteen locality pages covering connectivity, pricing trends, schools, infrastructure and what is being built.

02

Projects as inventory

Project pages now hang off their locality, so a completed project redirects its authority rather than disappearing.

03

Local presence

Claimed and structured the sales-office profiles, with review generation tied to the site-visit flow.

04

Comparison content

Honest locality-versus-locality comparisons, including the ones where Nilaya does not build.

03 · What we changed

The work, in four buckets.

01

Technical SEO

Replaced the pattern that 404’d sold-out projects with redirects into their locality page, preserving nine years of accumulated links.

02

Content

Built fourteen locality pages and eleven comparison pages, each updated quarterly with current pricing data.

03

Local SEO

Claimed and structured four sales-office profiles, and tied review requests to the post-visit follow-up.

04

Site structure

Reorganised projects under localities so authority accumulates in the pages that persist.

05 · Business impact

An asset that survives the project cycle

The commercial point of locality pages is that they do not expire. A project sells out; the locality page keeps ranking and routes buyers to whatever is launching next.

Two new launches in month seven reached their first-month site-visit target without the paid campaign that previous launches had required.

Because locality pages carry honest comparisons, the buyers arriving at a site visit are better informed, which the sales team reports as shorter negotiations.
