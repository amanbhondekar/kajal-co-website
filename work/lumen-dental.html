Our Works / Performance Marketing / Lumen Dental
Healthcare · Performance Marketing

Cost per booked consult down to ₹640

Tying local campaigns for a dental group to the booking calendar rather than to clicks, with creative refreshed fortnightly.

Client
Lumen Dental
Industry
Dental · Multi-clinic
Channels
Google Ads, Meta Ads
Duration
4 months
Objective
Booked consultations, not leads
01 · The challenge

Plenty of leads, empty chairs

Three clinics were generating form fills at a respectable cost and converting very few of them into attended appointments. The campaigns were optimising for the wrong event.

  • Cost per lead looked healthy; cost per attended consult was never measured
  • Form fills went to a shared inbox and were followed up when someone had time
  • All three clinics shared one campaign, so the nearest location was often not the one advertised
  • Creative ran unchanged for months in a small local audience
02 · Campaign strategy

Optimise for the calendar, not the form

We moved the conversion event from form submission to confirmed booking, then let the platforms optimise against the thing the business actually wanted.

Audience
Within 6km of each clinic, split by treatment intent — routine, cosmetic and orthodontic
Offer
Free first consultation with a named clinician and a visible available slot
Creative
Clinician-to-camera video per clinic, refreshed fortnightly
Google SearchGoogle LocalMeta AdsCalendar-integrated booking
Step 01 Ad

Location-specific creative naming the nearest clinic and the clinician.

Step 02 Landing page

One page per clinic, showing live availability rather than a contact form.

Step 03 Action

Slot picked and confirmed on the page, with an SMS confirmation.

Step 04 Conversion

Booking event sent to both platforms, and an attendance event sent the following day.

03 · Execution

What actually went live.

01

Campaign structure

Split one shared campaign into three location campaigns, each with its own radius, budget and landing page.

02

Creative

Each clinic’s own clinician on camera, which outperformed the stock-photography set from the first week.

03

Landing pages

Replaced the enquiry form with live calendar availability, so the visitor’s next action was booking rather than waiting.

04

Measurement

Two conversion events — booking and attendance — so no-shows are visible instead of being counted as success.

04 · Optimisation

Decision, reason, outcome.

Decision

Moved the optimisation event to confirmed booking

Reason

Form fills were converting to attended consults at under a third.

Outcome

Lead volume fell; attended consultations rose in the same fortnight.

Decision

Split budget by clinic rather than evenly

Reason

One location had twice the search demand and a third of the budget.

Outcome

Cost per booking fell fastest at the under-funded clinic.

Decision

Paused cosmetic keywords during the first month

Reason

They carried the highest cost and the lowest attendance rate.

Outcome

Reintroduced in month three with a dedicated page and a realistic price band.

Decision

Added an SMS reminder 24 hours ahead

Reason

No-shows were concentrated in bookings made more than four days out.

Outcome

Attendance rate improved enough to change the effective cost per consult.

The numbers that moved.

Click-through rate 2.1% 4.4% +110%
Monthly booked consults 48 173 +260%

Month four against month zero. The “before” cost per booked consult is reconstructed from clinic records, since bookings were not tracked as a conversion at the time.

06 · Business result

A fuller calendar on roughly the same spend

Monthly spend rose only marginally. Almost all of the gain came from pointing the same budget at a better-defined outcome.

173 attended consultations a month against 48 is a capacity question rather than a marketing one, which is a better problem for the group to have.

Because attendance is now a tracked event, the clinics can see which treatments and which locations actually fill, and staff accordingly.

The campaign did not need more budget or better targeting. It needed to be optimised against the event the business cared about, which turned out to be two steps further down the funnel than anyone was measuring.
