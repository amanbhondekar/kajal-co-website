Project enquiries up 2.4× after launch

A quieter site for an architecture practice that lets the work make the argument, with a brief form that filters badly matched leads.

01 · The challenge

Good work, badly matched enquiries

Studio Mehr was not short of enquiries. It was short of the right ones. Most first calls ended on budget or scope, which cost the practice several hours a week and told prospective clients nothing before they picked up the phone.

The site showed finished photography and almost nothing about process, fees or how a project runs.

The contact form asked for a name and a message, so every enquiry arrived as an unknown quantity.

Fewer, better-matched enquiries — and a shorter path from first contact to a productive conversation.

02 · The strategy

Say the hard things earlier

A practice this size competes on fit, not on volume. The site’s job was to let people disqualify themselves before anyone spent an hour on a call.

Publish the process and the fee structure
Let project pages carry drawings, not just photographs
Replace the contact form with a short brief
Keep the interface quiet so the work stays loud
03 · The experience

The decisions that did the work.

Decision 01

A brief, not a contact form

The enquiry asks for site location, approximate budget band, timeline and whether planning permission is in place — four questions, each optional, on one screen.

Why it mattered

Giving people a budget band to choose from sets expectations without the practice having to name a number first. Enquiries that arrive now carry enough to decide whether a call is worth booking.

Decision 02

Drawings shown next to the photographs

Each project page pairs the finished photography with plans, sections and a short note on what the constraint was.

Why it mattered

Clients commissioning architecture are buying judgement. Showing the drawing alongside the result demonstrates it in a way a hero image cannot.

Decision 03

Fees and process made public

A plain page covers stages, what happens in each, roughly how long it takes and how fees are structured.

Why it mattered

It removed the single most common reason a first call went nowhere, and it reads as confidence rather than as a price list.

05 · Implementation

Static where it can be, managed where it must be

Built as a static site with a headless CMS behind it, so project pages load instantly and the studio can publish a new project without a developer.

  • Projects, drawings and process copy all editable by the studio
  • Image pipeline generates responsive sets on upload
  • Brief submissions routed to the principal’s inbox with a structured summary
  • No analytics beyond first-party page counts, at the practice’s request
