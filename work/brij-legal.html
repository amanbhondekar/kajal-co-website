01 · The challenge

Written for lawyers, found by nobody

Brij’s practice-area pages were written in the language of the statute. Prospective clients search in the language of the problem, and the two almost never matched.

  • Seven practice areas shared one page of about 200 words
  • No page addressed a question a non-lawyer would actually type
  • Partner profiles carried no practice context, so expertise was invisible to search
  • Referrals were the only meaningful source of new work
02 · SEO strategy

Write for the problem, not the statute

Legal buyers search a situation before they search a service. The content plan started from those situations and connected each one to the practice area that handles it.

01

Search intent

We mapped the questions clients ask in a first call and treated each as a page, rather than starting from the firm’s own service taxonomy.

02

Practice-area architecture

Each of the seven areas got a full page with scope, typical engagements, the partners who handle it and an indicative fee structure.

03

Authority

Partner commentary placed in trade publications, each linking back to the relevant practice area rather than to the homepage.

04

Technical base

Fixed the JavaScript-rendered navigation that was leaving most of the site undiscovered, and added professional-service structured data.

03 · What we changed

The work, in four buckets.

01

Technical SEO

Replaced the client-rendered navigation that had left 80% of pages undiscovered, and added structured data for the firm and its practitioners.

02

On-page SEO

Rebuilt seven practice-area pages and 23 partner profiles so each targets a defined set of queries.

03

Content

Published 41 situation-led guides, each written to answer one question completely and link to the practice area that handles it.

04

Authority

Placed partner commentary in eleven trade and business publications over the campaign, linking to practice areas rather than the homepage.

05 · Business impact

A second pipeline next to the referral network

The referral network did not change. What changed is that the firm now has a second source of work it can actually influence.

Because the guides address a situation and the practice-area page sets out scope and fees, enquiries arrive pre-sorted — partners spend less of a first call establishing what the matter even is.

Three of the seven practice areas now generate enough search enquiries to justify their own associate, which was the firm’s stated objective.
